    The code behind the line

    The whole workflow, read from scenario.py — the file running above. Eight nodes as data, one small class that teaches grampy to read a brick, and a handful of calls. Bricks come from two crates and take the same line: the factory ones are polished, the salvage ones are not, and that difference is stated once.

    What you are looking at

    • inbox is a Lane.throttle: a brick sent back waits there 30 s after its last pass — sent back again meanwhile, it keeps its place and takes the latest version — and never more than 90 s.
    • polish is optional, and the salvage crate (a blue dot on the brick) skips it: that channel gives the node a grace, so the janitor skips it and nothing downstream waits. A channel changes settings, never the shape of the workflow.
    • scan is a choice: it names its branch, and the branch not taken is marked omitted.
    • quarantine is a wait: the brick sits on the shelf until the bomb squad's signal arrives, or fails after its 90 s timeout.
    • defuse has a Retry(limit=3, exponential): a failure goes round the return belt and comes back later — 8 s, 16 s, 32 s.
    • reject accepts only failed parents (on=…, need=1): past the last retry, or when the squad never came, the brick goes to waste.
    • pack joins both routes: omitted satisfies it, so a clean brick and a defused one both land in the tray of their colour.
    • The dashed pads are waiting: a brick sits there until its node is claimable and a worker is free. Inside a tray, it is being worked.
